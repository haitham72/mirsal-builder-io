# Engine

<sub>Part of [engine-and-studio.md](../engine-and-studio.md).</sub>

Stills (`engine/sheet.py`, pure): cut the sheet with `engine/grid.py` (below) -> per cell: sample background from the 4 px border ring (median), colour-difference key `d = K - max(other two)` with `t = 0.5 x median(d on ring)` (alpha linear between `t/2` and `t`) -> drop specks < 64 px (no erosion) -> despill the edge band only -> trim to bbox -> **one pack-wide scale** (median fit, clamped so nothing exceeds 0.85 x 512) -> premultiplied linear resize -> centre on 512x512 -> white outline (12 px) -> validate. Checks: `dimensions, transparent_corners, foreground, inside_cell, no_spill, static_file` (PNG, else lossless WEBP if > 512 KB). `no_spill` counts key-coloured opaque pixels **on the edge band only** (outline + despill band + 3 px from transparency). Key-coloured pixels deeper inside are the subject's own colours: they go to `metrics.chroma_risk` (share of the subject), with `warnings: ["chroma_risk"]` above 3%. That is a warning for the human, never a block. Measured on the 10 real sheets: every pixel the old whole-subject rule failed on was interior, with 0 on the edge. Blank cell -> `empty_subject`; subject touching the cell edge -> `inside_cell`.

**Key-failure branch (`sheet.py`, stills).** A failed cell is never just dropped: (1) **dissect** - log why (border ring pollution, threshold, foreground and edge pixels); (2) **key again** on a bounded ladder chosen by the failure reason (`empty_subject`: sheet-wide background, then threshold x0.6; `no_spill`: threshold x0.75 + 6 px despill, then x0.6 + 10 px; other: sheet-wide background); `inside_cell` is ruled out at once because keying cannot fix a subject crossing its cell border; (3) **rule out** - `FAILED` with `metrics.ruled_out` and the full `metrics.attempts` log, which the console shows under "keying recovery". A future last rung is a matting re-key (see "Keyers"). Healthy cells are never retried.

**Grid (`engine/grid.py`).** Grids are 3x3 (default), 2x2, or 1x1 (one regenerated sticker).
- `detect_grid` counts the interior gutter bands: columns or rows that are at least 97% background and at least 1% of the side wide.
- `split_grid` cuts in the middle of the background band nearest each expected line. Where there is no clear band, that one cut falls back to equal division; `method` is `gutter | equal | mixed | single`.
- The rects are stored in `result.json` (`source.grid`), drawn as cut lines on the console's raw sheet, and reused for the 3x3 mp4: `scale_rects` maps them onto the video, since image-to-video keeps the layout. The live canvas preview uses the same rects.
- Real AI sheets put gutters up to ~90 px off the thirds. Cutting at thirds sliced 12 of 90 real cells (`inside_cell`); cutting at the gutters slices none.
- The prompter's `expand(task, grid)` returns one sticker per cell, and `validate_plan` infers the grid from the sticker count (9, 4, 1).

Video (`engine/video.py`): decode one cell at a time (`ffmpeg.decode_cell`: the frame is first converted to RGB with the colour **interpolated** to every pixel, `SWS_CHROMA` = `bicubic+full_chroma_int+accurate_rnd`, then cropped exactly to the measured rect, else thirds; ffmpeg's default copied each half-resolution colour sample of the provider's H.264 to 2x2 pixels, so the key cut the edge in 2-pixel steps that the ~1.3x enlargement of a returned video sheet showed as a pixelated outline, 2026-10-09; `CACHE_VERSION` 6); native fps (<= 30, never upsampled), <= 3 s; background sampled **once** from the first frame; **one transform per clip**: with **Keep approved size** on (`anim_keep_size`, the default since 2026-10-12, the batch's `keep_size` switch on Motion) the first frame's subject is mapped onto the approved still's subject, same longest side and same centre (`keep_window` / `render_kept`), so the animation starts exactly as the still that was approved and keeps that size however far it moves; what moves past the 512 frame is cut off (`cut_off_frames`, a metric). The video sheet already fits every sticker to one scale, and fitting each clip to its whole motion again shrank the lively ones (G146: 🤣 and 😠 played at 0.86-0.88x while the rest were at 1.1-1.25x). Off, or with no still to match, the clip is fitted to its union bbox (the old way). On top of either, **Video size** (`anim_scale`, 0.5-1.5, about the canvas centre) scales the animation after the video is received: the batch's value (Motion's batch row, `result.anim_scale`) for all of its animations, or a sticker's own (`−` / `+` under its tile, `sticker.anim_scale`), which wins; `pipeline.cell_cfgs` groups the cells by the size they are cut with, so each size has its own cache key. Every change cuts again from the stored video, free; loop close: if the last->first `loop_seam` exceeds `max(12, 1.0 x the clip's own median frame-to-frame change)` (`loop_seam_ratio`; it was 1.5, which let a lively clip snap at the wrap: G146 S1-S4, 2026-10-12), the clip first **ends on the frame that leads back to frame 0** (`loop_end`: the latest end in its last 30% whose wrap is within the limit, never repeating frame 0; a cut blends nothing, so nothing turns see-through; Haitham, 2026-10-05: the cross-fade left a half-transparent double image, a grey seam, at every wrap), and only a jump that is still left is closed by cross-fading the last 6 frames into the first ones — **blended in premultiplied alpha and un-premultiplied after**, so a transparent tail frame cannot drag the character's colour towards black (a straight per-channel RGBA blend did exactly that and showed as a GREY FRAME at the wrap, once per loop; Haitham, 2026-10-03; `close_loop`; `CACHE_VERSION` 5 since `loop_end`). `python -m mirsal reloop [G### ... | all]` makes existing animations again from their stored videos with the current loop and refreshes their pack copies (free; it preserves human reviews and the completed pack stage when previously ready animations stay ready, skips Studio-edited batches or missing stored videos, reports skips/errors, and needs the server stopped: one writer of result.json); encode `libvpx-vp9 yuva420p` with CRF ladder 30/38/46/54/60 until <= 256 KB. If the ffmpeg in use has no libvpx-vp9 (Anaconda's has none, and it also rejects `-deadline`), every requested cell fails at once with `no_vp9_encoder` and the fix in `metrics.error`, instead of a bare `exception` per cell (which is what happened to every earlier real run). Checks: `size_budget, codec_vp9, dimensions, fps, duration, no_audio, alpha_mode_tag, alpha_decoded` (decoded with `-c:v libvpx-vp9`, since the default decoder hides alpha), `loop_seam`.

**Pre-sliced clips** skip the keying step: alpha haze below `clip_alpha_floor` (12) is zeroed (VP9 alpha leaves values 1-3 around the subject), specks are dropped, the edge band is despilled, then the same transform / loop close / encode / validation as above. `edge_touch_frames` is reported (warning only): it counts frames whose subject touches the slice border, meaning the slice may clip the character.
